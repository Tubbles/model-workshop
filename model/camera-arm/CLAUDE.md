# CLAUDE.md

Guidance for Claude Code working on the camera arm, in `model/camera-arm/`.

## What this is

`model/camera-arm/scripts/export_camera_arm.py` exports a Raspberry Pi Camera Module 2 arm for the user's stock Ender 3 V2 from Part primitives with no STEP input, four STLs into a given directory, each lying on z = 0 the way it prints without support (`Camera_Arm`, `Camera_T_Nut`, `Camera_Case_Back`, `Camera_Case_Front`). The arm stands on the top face of the base's right 4040 rail behind the right upright, clamped by an M3 x 10 into the printed twist-in T-nut (captive M3 nut) in the outer top slot, with ribs in both slots' mouths to keep it square, and holds the case at one fixed angle (`CAMERA_YAW_DEGREES`, `CAMERA_PITCH_DEGREES`, `HEAD_BAR_BACK_CENTER_MM`). The two shell case closes with four M2 screws through the board's holes into heat-set inserts in the back shell, fixes to the arm with two M2 screws through the back shell's ears into inserts in the arm, and takes a cable sock's end in an 18.4 x 8.8 mm socket at the ribbon exit. The board numbers come from Raspberry Pi's mechanical drawing, the field of view from Raspberry Pi's documentation, the travel from Creality's V2 firmware, and the frame, bed, slot, upright and gantry numbers from Creality's Ender-3 CAD, since Creality publishes none for the V2; the docstring cites each and lists what is assumed (board thickness, component heights, ribbon width). The arm is built in its own print frame: a profile in the vertical plane of the camera's viewing direction, extruded along the case's width and clipped at that frame's bed plane, so its axis-aligned base gets 45 degree faces at worst. The script checks each part is one valid solid with a closed mesh, probes its key features (the arm's bolt hole, counterbore and bar insert bores among them), checks the T-nut's margins in the slot (neck, turn, plate overlap on the lips, bolt engagement), flags planar faces facing down past 45 degrees in the print orientation, checks the board model, shells and arm do not overlap and a 5 mm straight driver reaches the ear screws past the front shell, measures the clearance to the upright, the gantry's right end, the bed and knobs over the travel and the print volume, and prints how much of the print area the lens sees at each bed Y position, plain and with the upright, the gantry end and the X beam at their lowest as occluders. The docstring gives the assembly order, which the layout depends on: the back shell sits over the M3 bolt's axis, so the arm goes on the rail before the case goes on the arm (afterwards only an L key reaches the bolt), and the T-nut is turned by the bolt while pulled up against the lips, since loose on the slot's floor it has no room to turn.

It imports `board_step.py` from `model/kbdkid4/scripts/` for the STL export with its closed mesh check and for the freecadcmd scaffolding. The freecadcmd quirks are in the root [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) (FreeCAD scripts). Read them before changing the script.

`work/camera-arm.md` (design notes) and `work/research/camera/` (the downloaded drawings, manual, firmware `Configuration.h` and Creality's Ender-3 CAD, the downloads the docstring's sources point to) are local, untracked copies in the repository root's `work/`.

## CI

The `camera-arm` job in `.github/workflows/ci.yml` exports the four STLs from the repository root into `camera/` (`--pass camera`) and uploads them as the `camera-arm-stl-<short commit hash>` artifact. The hash is this repository's commit, the pull request head's for pull requests (`github.sha` is a merge commit there).
