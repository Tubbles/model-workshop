# kbdkid5 design

kbdkid5 is the successor of [kbdkid4](https://github.com/Tubbles/kbdkid4): a wireless split keyboard (Kailh Choc v1 on hotswap sockets, 19.05 mm pitch, a nice!nano v2 and a LiPo per half, ZMK), with a 3D-printed tray and switch plate. Its two changes:

1. **Nothing but the switch matrix on the PCB.** The PCB carries only the hotswap sockets, the diodes and one off-board connector. The nice!nano, the reset button and the power switch are wired with loose wires and mounted in the tray itself, so the tray can use the space under the PCB. The battery is the user's to place and wire (Parts in the tray, Battery).
2. **A slanted inner edge to join the halves.** The tray, PCB and plate are cut straight from the thumb key's top right corner to the rightmost top row key's top right corner. The two halves join along that slant with magnets and steel dowel pins into one Alice-like board (each half turned 17.7 degrees, rows dipping toward the middle), firm enough to type on in the lap.

The PCB itself is designed later (LibrePCB). Until then the geometry here is the ground truth, held in `model/kbdkid5/scripts/layout.py`, and the PCB is a printed mock. Part choices and their sources are in [doc/components.md](../../doc/components.md).

## Frame and units

Millimetres. The left half in kbdkid4's board coordinates: x east, y north, the board's top side (keys) up, heights z above the tray floor's top face (the floor's underside is at z -1.2). The right half is the left half mirrored across the vertical line x = 66.199, the outline's center line, so a right part lies where its left one does. Two things differ: the joint's dowel holes (see below), and the switch footprints with their sockets, since a switch's two pins are not mirror symmetric. On the right half each key's footprint is the left half's, unmirrored, at the mirrored key center, as on kbdkid4's right half (its board turned over) seen from above, so its sockets reach toward the slant instead of away from it. This is a constraint on the PCB design: the right tray is cut for exactly these sockets (Tray) and the joint is placed to clear them (The joint), so a right half switch turned any other way, say 180 degrees, needs the right tray's cuts and the joint worked out again.

## Parts in the tray

Every part sits in a socket printed into the tray that locates it and takes its working forces: the power switch's slide and a push on its knob, the reset button's press, the nice!nano's USB-C plug going in. A blob of hot glue on top only keeps a part down against vibration. The user, 2026-10-21: "I don't want to rely solely on glue for fitting parts into the tray. There needs to be sockets in the print where the components goes, and then as a vibration surviving measure I put a blob of hot glue on top to keep it down."

Every bought part also has to come out of an assembled keyboard undamaged and go into a new set of printed parts, so nothing fixes a part into the print in a way a new print cannot reuse it: no permanent glue, and no fit the part cannot leave undamaged. Wires soldered to the parts are expected: the wired parts move to the new print together as one harness, the nice!nano coming off its socket strips. The heat-set inserts are the one part that stays in the old print. The user, 2026-10-21: "Also I foresee I will iterate quickly on various model designs so I can just migrate all the parts from a fully assembled keyboard into a new set of printed parts, like the option of adding the thumb joystick down the line", and "Some soldering is fine, like we need to solder into the switches' hotswap sockets".

Envelopes below are bodies only. `build_tray()` builds every part's mounts into both trays, each mount a box in `model/kbdkid5/scripts/layout.py` (`MountBox`), the right half's in the left half's frame and mirrored with the tray (Mirroring, below). ## The joint

The slanted tray wall's outer face, 1.2 out of the slant (the gap and the wall), is the joint face. Joined, the two halves' outer slant faces touch, each half turned 17.74 degrees. The pins and magnets sit at the same places along the slant on both halves, since the halves mate face to face. The joint face's bottom edge is chamfered 0.4 at 45 degrees along the whole slant, running out on the corner rounds, so elephant foot cannot keep the faces apart along the bed.

Every printed fit, in the joint and elsewhere, is a snug, a slip or a glued fit by the print model (doc/print-model.md, The model the scripts use), which also gives every figure "as printed".

- **Block:** below the PCB, the slanted wall is 4.0 thick (normal to the face) from the floor up to the ledge top (z 9.5), replacing the ledge there. The PCB rests on it.
- **Magnets:** four supermagnete S-08-03-N (8 x 3, N45) per half, axis normal to the face, standing on edge, each held by hot glue against a 0.5 skin at the face, which takes its pull. Each slot is the magnet's glued fit, 8.5 along the slant and 3.5 across (8.1 and 3.1 printed), from z -0.7 up, 0.5 above the bed. The skin and the slot fill the block's 4.0, so the slot is a notch through the block, open at the top and toward the cavity, with no overhang to print. The PCB covers the slots.
