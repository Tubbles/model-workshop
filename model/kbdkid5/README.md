# kbdkid5

A wireless split keyboard with Kailh Choc v1 switches on hotswap sockets and a nice!nano v2 per half, the successor of [kbdkid4](https://github.com/Tubbles/kbdkid4). The PCB carries only the switch matrix. The nice!nano, power switch, reset button and battery connector are wired by hand and mounted in the 3D-printed tray, which makes the half smaller than kbdkid4's. A slanted inner edge lets the two halves join with magnets and steel dowel pins into one Alice-like board.

The design, with every dimension, is in [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md). The part choices and their sources are in [doc/components.md](../../doc/components.md), and the plan is in [PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md). The PCB itself is not designed yet. For now a printed mock stands in for it.

CI runs each export below with the command shown, from this repository's root, in the image built from `docker/freecad.Dockerfile`, and uploads the STLs as artifacts named after the commit.

## 3D printed PCB mock

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_pcb.py` exports a printable stand-in for the PCB until the real one is designed: the outline from `model/kbdkid5/scripts/layout.py` (the geometry of DESIGN.md as data) extruded to 1.6 mm, with every key's Choc hotswap footprint holes and the mounting drills, and no sockets. It test-fits the printed tray and plate. To mount switches, print the fused mock below. Printed parts come out slightly larger than modelled, so the mock is pulled in by `pull_in` (default 0.2 mm) per side: the outline moves inward and every hole grows outward by that much.

    docker build -t freecad-headless -f docker/freecad.Dockerfile docker
    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_pcb.py --pass pcb.stl [right] [pull_in=0.2]

CI first checks the layout with `python3 model/kbdkid5/scripts/check_layout.py` (outline, keys, drills, the parts in the tray, the sockets against the tray on both halves, the joint), then uploads both halves as the `kbdkid5-pcb-stl-<short commit hash>` artifact: `kbdkid5_Pcb_Left.stl` and `kbdkid5_Pcb_Right.stl` (the word `right`). The right one has the outline and the drills mirrored across the outline's center line, but not the switch footprints, since a switch cannot be mirrored: each sits as on the left half, at the mirrored key center.

## 3D printed switch plate

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_plate.py` exports the switch plate: kbdkid3's plate (one Choc cell per key) aligned onto the layout's keys, trimmed 0.4 mm at its outer edges to fit the tray, the switch-free corner by the slant covered up to 0.35 mm past the outline's slant, and 4.17 mm holes over the screw heads at the mounting drills. kbdkid4's print corrections apply: the hole pattern moves `pattern_shift` (default 0.25 mm) along x while the outline stays put, and `side_shave` (default 0.1 mm) comes off the west and east edges and the slanted edge. The word `covered` makes the plate for a half without the hard-to-reach key S123: its cutout is filled in and a pit in the underside clears its diode. The plate is written upside down, its top face on the bed.

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_plate.py --pass plate.stl [right] [covered] \
        [pattern_shift=0.25] [side_shave=0.1] [hole_diameter=4.17]

CI uploads both halves, plain and covered, as the `kbdkid5-plate-stl-<short commit hash>` artifact: `kbdkid5_Plate_Left.stl`, `kbdkid5_Plate_Right.stl`, `kbdkid5_Plate_Left_Covered.stl` and `kbdkid5_Plate_Right_Covered.stl`. The right ones are mirrored across the outline's center line.

## 3D printed fused mock

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_mock.py` exports the PCB mock and the switch plate fused into one part, to mount switches in and get a feel of the layout. Each key's two pin holes become narrow slits in the PCB layer that grip the switch's flat pin blades (0.2 x 0.8 mm, as measured on kbdkid4's switch model): `slit_width` across the blade, default 0.6 mm (the blade plus `pull_in` per side), and `slit_length` along it, default 1.6 mm (the blade plus `pull_in` per side plus 0.4 mm, so only the width grips). `pull_in` sets those defaults but does not grow a given `slit_width` or `slit_length`. The plate has no `pattern_shift`, since the plate and the PCB layer are one print. The part is written PCB side down. Check in the slicer preview that every slit is still open in the first layers, since elephant foot and XY or hole compensation change slits this small. The pins, the center post and the pegs reach 1.4 mm below the part, so a mounted switch needs it raised off the table.

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_mock.py --pass mock.stl [right] \
        [pull_in=0.2] [slit_width=0.6] [slit_length=1.6]

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_slit_test.py` exports a strip of six single-key cells of the PCB layer alone, with no plate, to find the slits that grip the switch's pins. The first strip's slits, 0.5 to 1.0 mm, all let the blades through easily, so the fused mock's default 0.6 mm does not grip either. On a real switch the two blades are not equally thick, so each pin gets its own slit, its blade's thickness plus the cell's clearance as modelled (no `pull_in`), from -0.15 mm in cell 1 to +0.10 mm in cell 6 in 0.05 mm steps: 0.05 to 0.30 mm for the middle pin, 0.25 to 0.50 mm for the pin near a side peg, all 1.6 mm long. PrusaSlicer's "Slice gap closing radius" defaults to 0.049 mm, which fills cell 1's middle slit, so lower that setting for this strip, and check every slit in the slicer preview. Elephant foot on the bed face matters too, as for the fused mock. Cell k has k notches in its south edge. The clearance that holds a switch best sets the fused mock's slits. `wide` exports the wide strip instead, the first strip's widths again without its plate: 0.5 mm in cell 1 to 1.0 mm in cell 6 in 0.1 mm steps, as modelled and the same on both pins of a cell, all 1.6 mm long. The notches are the same on both strips. On the wide one both slits of a cell are equally wide, which tells them apart.

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_slit_test.py --pass strip.stl [wide] [pull_in=0.2]

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_socket_test.py` exports three stackable test strips for a keyboard without a PCB: a printed frame as thick as the PCB takes its place, each hotswap socket held in a pocket on the frame's underside, and the switches wired by hand. Each strip has six cells, and cell k has k notches in its south edge. `pocket` steps the pocket's fit from -0.40 to -0.15 mm, its two pin holes 3.5 mm so the socket's posts cannot bind and only the pocket holds it (five notches in its north edge, one at every cell boundary, where the third pocket strip had four, the second two and the first, -0.10 to +0.15, none). Its pocket walls follow the socket's own outline, curves and all, where the earlier strips went around a cover of rectangles that stood off the socket at the step between its two lobes. The walls are built whole and the switch's post and peg holes are drilled through them, so on the wall nearest the switch's center the peg's hole leaves a sliver 0.1 to 0.2 mm thick on the pocket side, which printed whole on the third strip. `holes` has loose pockets (+0.15 mm) and steps the two pin holes from 3.5 to 3.0 mm, so only the holes hold the socket (three notches in its north edge). `backing` screws under either frame strip with M2 screws into heat-set inserts in the frame's bosses, its pads pressing each socket from -0.10 (a gap) to +0.15 mm (the third backing, with pads 0.1 mm smaller each way than the second's and 0.3 mm smaller than the first's, has two notches in its north edge; the second had one and the first none). The pocket strip's best cell's fit, -0.15 mm (cell 6 of the fourth strip), is the frame's pocket fit. Solder a wire to each socket tab before pressing the socket in: the tab and its wire slide up a slot in the pocket wall. The frame strips print upside down, the backing bottom down. `insert_length` sets the bores for inserts shorter than the longest the bosses hold, 3.067 mm (an M2 x 3 fits).

`switch` exports the switch hole strip instead, to find the holes for the switch's center post and side pegs that keep it from wobbling sideways, since the socket strips' holes are too wide for that. It is the frame's body alone, six cells, 114.3 x 19.05 x 1.6 mm, with no pockets or bosses. Cells 1 to 5 step the post's and both pegs' holes together so that they print 0.05 mm tight, exact, and 0.05, 0.10 and 0.15 mm loose on a 3.17 mm post and 1.65 mm pegs: 3.12 to 3.32 mm for the post and 1.60 to 1.80 mm for the pegs as printed, 0.30 mm more as modelled. Cell 6 has the socket strips' holes (3.5 and 2.0 mm as printed) for reference. The holes the switch's pin blades pass through are 3.2 mm, so only the post and the pegs hold the switch. The printed sizes take a hole to print 0.15 mm per side smaller than modelled, as measured on the hole strip, which is unmeasured for holes as small as the pegs', so the pegs may come out different. It prints top face down. On the user's print cell 1 fit best, its holes hugging the post and pegs in a tight friction fit, so a real frame's post and peg holes are cell 1's (3.42 and 1.90 mm as modelled).

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_socket_test.py --pass strip.stl pocket|backing|holes|switch \
        [pull_in=0.2] [insert_length=<mm>]

To try the pocket strip, push a socket into each pocket by hand from the open (wall) side. A good pocket takes it with a light push, lets it sit flat against the ceiling, keeps it in when the strip is turned over and tapped, and does not let it shift when nudged sideways. Loose means it falls out or rattles, too tight means it needs a tool or will not sit flat. The pocket alone does not have to survive a switch being pressed in: the backing does that. Report the cell number (its south notches) of the best one, or "all loose" or "all tight".

To try the hole strip, push a socket in so its two posts enter the holes. The pocket is loose on purpose and will not hold anything. The best cell is the tightest whose posts still go fully in by hand and sit flat, and whose socket stays in when the strip is turned over and tapped. Report the best cell, or "all loose" or "all tight".

To try the switch hole strip, push a switch into each cell from the face that was on the bed and wiggle it sideways. A good cell takes the switch fully in by hand, lets it sit flat and holds it without play sideways. Loose means it wobbles, too tight means it will not go fully in or sit flat. Report the tightest cell the switch goes fully into by hand and sits flat in, which cells still let it wobble, and whether the post or the pegs seem to bind first.

CI uploads both halves of the fused mock and the slit strips as the `kbdkid5-mock-stl-<short commit hash>` artifact: `kbdkid5_Mock_Left.stl`, `kbdkid5_Mock_Right.stl`, `kbdkid5_Slit_Test.stl` and `kbdkid5_Slit_Test_Wide.stl`. The socket strips have their own job and artifact, `kbdkid5-socket-stl-<short commit hash>`: `kbdkid5_Socket_Test_Pocket.stl`, `kbdkid5_Socket_Test_Holes.stl`, `kbdkid5_Socket_Test_Backing.stl` and `kbdkid5_Switch_Hole_Test.stl`, ready a few minutes after a push. The right half's outline and plate are mirrored like the other right parts, but not its switch footprints, since a switch cannot be mirrored: each sits as on the left half, at the mirrored key center.

## 3D printed tray

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_tray.py` exports a half's tray: walls, floor and the ledge the PCB rests on, standoffs bored for M2 heat-set inserts at the mounting drills, the cuts that clear the switches' hotswap sockets, and the joint along the slanted edge. The joint has four slots for 8 x 3 mm magnets, open at the top and toward the inside, each magnet glued against the thin skin at the joint face, and two flat-topped teardrop holes for 4 x 16 mm steel dowel pins: a snug fit on the left half, so a pin pressed in by hand stays put and stops there, and a slip fit with a lead-in on the right. Every fit is modelled 0.2 mm per side larger than it should print, for the print's swelling: 4.4 mm for the snug fit's 4.0 mm, 4.5 mm for the slip fit's 4.1 mm. The joint face's bottom edge is chamfered against elephant foot. A parking hole in the thumb key's east wall holds a pin while the halves are used apart. The tray holds the parts that are not on the PCB. The nice!nano, with Mill-Max socket strips soldered over its pin rows, lies on a seat 1.2 mm over the floor in a pocket between the west ledge and a tall east wall, with channels beside the seat for the strips' solder tails and the ledge and the wall cut back over the strips. It goes in nose first, its USB-C shell under the bar of a block across its USB end at the USB window, and its far end comes down past a foot as tall as the board, where a printed tab holds it, screwed down with an M2 x 4 screw into an M2 x 3 heat-set insert in a block south of the board. A 5 mm pry slot through that block and the foot, with the seat left out under it, lets a toothpick under the board's south end to lift it out once the tab is off. No hole goes through the floor. The power switch sits in a cradle, its lever through a slot in the north wall. The reset button rests against a back wall and is pressed with a SIM pin through a hole in the north wall. The battery connector sits in a pocket, the battery between L-shaped ribs at three of its corners. Wires keep room behind every lead. The export checks the tray against DESIGN.md's figures and, joined with the other half, that the pin holes line up and nothing overlaps. The tray is written floor on the bed.

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_tray.py --pass tray.stl [right] [pin_hole_diameter=<mm>] \
        [nano_length=33.2] [nano_width=18.2] [nano_pocket_fit=0.1] [clamp_press=0.1] \
        [bar_clearance=0.1] [battery_shoulder=0]

For a test print, `pin_hole_diameter` overrides the half's pin hole fit as modelled, and the others the figures the mounts rest on: the nice!nano's board length and width (the defaults are the user's caliper, so these suit another board), how much wider than the board its pocket prints, how hard the clamp's tab presses the board (how far below the tab's seat on the board its block's top stands), the bar's clearance over the USB-C shell, and lips reaching that far onto the battery header's front rim (none by default, DESIGN.md says why). Glue the battery header into its pocket and the reset button to the floor. The right tray is mirrored across the outline's center line like the other right parts, but cut for the right half's sockets, which sit as on the left half at the mirrored key centers.

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_clamp_tab.py` exports the nice!nano clamp's tab, the same for both halves and the test bay below, printed top face down. Press the insert into the block south of the nice!nano, lay the board in nose first, put the tab on with its nose over the board's south edge and screw it down.

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_clamp_tab.py --pass tab.stl

`model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_nano_bay_test.py` exports one nice!nano bay cut out of the left tray, to settle the bay on a real board before printing whole trays: the seat, its channels and the strips' pockets, the pocket at the default slip fit, 0.1 mm wider and longer than the board as printed, the clamp's press and the bar's clearance at their defaults, and one notch in its south edge. A single bay keeps the print short (the user's choice). `BAY_FITS` in the script can step the fit over several bays again. `nano_length` and `nano_width` fit the bay to a calipered board.

To try it, solder the socket strips on first, then drop the board in nose first and screw the tab on: an M2 x 4, or on the test bay an M2 x 6 with an M2 nut under its head (the trays take only the M2 x 4). Push a USB-C cable in and pull it out: the board should not move or ride up. Wiggle the board across and along: loose means it rattles, too tight means it would not drop in flat on the seat. Then take the tab off and lift the board out with a toothpick through the slot's east part, x 3.6 to 6.4 mm, the 2.8 mm beside the face nearest the screw, so it passes the last solder tail of the board's west row. Note whether the board went in and sat flat, whether it is loose, snug or tight across and along, whether the strips and their tails touched anything, whether it held under the cable, and whether the toothpick got it out.

    docker run --rm -v "$PWD":/work -w /work freecad-headless \
        freecadcmd model/kbdkid5/scripts/export_nano_bay_test.py --pass bay.stl \
        [nano_length=33.2] [nano_width=18.2]

CI uploads both halves, the tab and the test bay as the `kbdkid5-tray-stl-<short commit hash>` artifact: `kbdkid5_Tray_Left.stl`, `kbdkid5_Tray_Right.stl`, `kbdkid5_Nano_Clamp_Tab.stl` and `kbdkid5_Nano_Bay_Test.stl`.
